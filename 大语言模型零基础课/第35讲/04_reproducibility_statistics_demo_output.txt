PART A: One experiment can mislead
==============================================================================
Single benchmark seed=3: A=0.640 B=0.670 diff(B-A)=+0.030

PART B: Repeat many independent benchmarks
==============================================================================
Mean diff(B-A) over 100 experiments = +0.031
Std of diff = 0.064
A wins=29, B wins=66, ties=5

PART C: Paired bootstrap CI on one benchmark
==============================================================================
seed=42 A=0.800 B=0.710 diff=-0.090
paired bootstrap 95% CI for diff = [-0.200, +0.020]

PART D: Seed reproduces a pseudo-random sequence
==============================================================================
seed=1: ['A', 'B', 'B', 'A', 'A', 'A', 'B', 'B', 'A', 'A', 'B', 'A']
seed=2: ['C', 'C', 'A', 'A', 'B', 'B', 'B', 'A', 'B', 'B', 'A', 'A']
seed=1 again: ['A', 'B', 'B', 'A', 'A', 'A', 'B', 'B', 'A', 'A', 'B', 'A']

Lesson:
- A better true method can still lose on one finite benchmark.
- Repeated trials estimate variability.
- Paired bootstrap puts uncertainty directly on the difference.
- Seed controls pseudo-random draws, not the entire software/hardware world.
